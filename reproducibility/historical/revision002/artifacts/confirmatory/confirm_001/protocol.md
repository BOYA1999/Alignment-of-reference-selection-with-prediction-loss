## 9. Estimators and exact formulas

For row `i`, let `L_i = |Y_i - prediction_i|`, `R_i` be availability, `p_i` a fitted or oracle propensity, and `m_i` the held-out loss-nuisance prediction.

- full truth: `T = mean(L_i)`;
- complete case: `CC = sum(R_i L_i) / sum(R_i)`;
- outcome regression: `OR = mean(m_i)`;
- Hajek IPW: `IPW = sum(R_i L_i / p_i) / sum(R_i / p_i)`;
- augmentation: `A_i = R_i (L_i - m_i) / p_i`;
- pseudo-outcome: `Q_i = m_i + A_i`;
- AIPW: `mean(Q_i)`;
- centered influence: `phi_i = Q_i - mean(Q)`;
- weighted ESS: `(sum(R_i/p_i))^2 / sum((R_i/p_i)^2)`; every reported ESS fraction is `ESS / sum(R_i)`.

With `G` scaffold groups and `S_g = sum(phi_i in group g)`, the cluster standard error is

`SE = sqrt((G/(G-1)) * sum_g S_g^2) / N`,

followed by the two-sided normal 95% interval `AIPW +/- 1.96*SE`. Coverage is inclusive: `lower <= T <= upper`.

Let `p_raw` be the held-out logistic probability before clipping and `p = clip(p_raw, 0.05, 0.95)`. The fitted clipping fraction is defined exactly as

`clip_fraction = N^(-1) sum_i 1[p_raw_i < 0.05 OR p_raw_i > 0.95]`.

The inequalities are strict; a raw probability equal to 0.05 or 0.95 is not counted as clipped. ESS, weights, IPW, AIPW, SMD, and maximum-weight diagnostics use `p` after clipping.

For each continuous low-dimensional audit feature `x`, use its original, unscaled target-cohort value in the frozen physical unit; never use or concatenate fold-specific standardized held-out values. `StandardScaler` is only a model-fitting transform. Weighted imbalance is

`SMD_x = |[sum_i R_i x_i/p_i / sum_i R_i/p_i] - [sum_i x_i/N]| / sd_full,ddof=0(x)`.

Zero-variance features are excluded from the maximum but recorded. `max_abs_SMD` is the maximum over the remaining 11 audit features.

The maximum normalized observed weight is

`max_i:R_i=1 [(R_i/p_i) / sum_j(R_j/p_j)]`.

Every median uses the ordinary midpoint convention. Every fifth or 95th percentile uses `numpy.quantile(..., method="linear")` on the complete finite vector. No percentile is rounded before a gate comparison.

Every ordered-vector SHA-256 uses one of four exact byte encodings with no metadata prefix: floating vectors use `np.asarray(v, dtype="<f8").tobytes(order="C")`; fold and source-row integer vectors use `np.asarray(v, dtype="<i8").tobytes(order="C")`; binary `R`, `formal_hit`, and availability vectors use `np.asarray(v, dtype="u1").tobytes(order="C")`; ordered text vectors, including canonical SMILES and Murcko group strings, use `"\n".join(str(v) for v in values).encode("utf-8")`, with LF separators and no terminal LF. Generator `p_oracle` and both PXR specifications' `p_raw` and clipped-`p` vectors use the floating-vector encoding. These rules apply wherever the same vector is hashed in an audit table, summary, manifest, or verifier.

All formula replays use absolute tolerance `1e-12`.

## 11. Admission and overlap gates

Passing H1/H2 establishes a replicated failure mechanism; it does not automatically authorize correction of a direct or genuinely sparse endpoint.

### 11.1 Known-truth admission of the candidate specification

The sole candidate eligible for admission is `P = lowdim_11, M = lowdim_11`. For every sentinel x regular anchor x prediction model:

- under MCAR, median AIPW absolute estimation error is at most 125% of the corresponding complete-case median absolute estimation error; MCAR has no selection bias to correct, so this is a non-inferiority guard rather than an improvement claim;
- under MAR, median AIPW absolute estimation error is at most 75% of the corresponding complete-case median absolute estimation error;
- empirical 95% interval coverage is at least 0.90;
- the fifth percentile of fitted-propensity ESS/observed is at least 0.50;
- median fitted clipping fraction is at most 0.10 and its 95th percentile is at most 0.20;
- the 95th percentile of `max_abs_SMD` is at most 0.10;
- every fit satisfies the fold class-support and finite-output rules.

All 2 sentinels x 2 regular anchors x 2 models x 2 mechanisms = 16 cells must pass. This is a necessary, not sufficient, gate for interpreting a fitted-propensity correction.

The two error criteria are evaluated as the explicit ratios `B_AIPW/B_CC`. If `B_CC` is zero or non-finite, or the ratio is non-finite, that cell is `NONADJUDICATIVE_FAIL`; no continuity correction is added.

### 11.2 Direct empirical-mask translation

All 64 direct empirical-mask factorial rows are always computed and written, regardless of admission status. If the candidate specification is not admitted, the translation gate is recorded as `NOT_ADJUDICATED_ADMISSION_FAILED` and none of those fitted-propensity estimates is interpreted as accepted correction. If it is admitted, adjudicate its direct empirical-mask performance on the two regular anchors. Across the 2 sentinels x 2 anchors x 2 models = 8 fixed cells, require:

`1 - median(abs(AIPW - T)) / median(abs(CC - T)) >= 0.25`

and AIPW absolute estimation error lower than complete-case absolute estimation error in at least 6 of 8 cells.

A zero or non-finite complete-case median-error denominator makes this translation gate non-adjudicative; it is not converted into a pass.

Failure is retained and blocks any claim that the candidate corrects real endpoint availability. The HPPB/RPPB stress masks never enter this gate and cannot rescue it.

### 11.3 Direct-mask overlap eligibility

For every reported fitted-propensity direct-mask estimate, separately record:

- observed and unavailable counts by nuisance fold;
- fitted clipping fraction;
- ESS/observed;
- `max_abs_SMD`;
- maximum normalized observed weight.
